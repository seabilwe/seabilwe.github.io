Key takeaways

  • Built-up land doubled, from 1,214 to 2,443 km², while the population nearly tripled, from 5.3 to 15.5 million.
  • So Gauteng got denser: built area per person fell from 100 to 72 m². Johannesburg's fell from 94 to 55; Midvaal's rose from 154 to 224.
  • 1990–2000 was the fastest decade, at about 43 km² of new built-up land a year.
  • 60% of new land was added at the edge of existing settlement; growth followed main roads more than railways.

Method

Built-up surface comes from GHSL's GHS-BUILT-S (R2023A) at 100 m. A hectare counts as built-up land once a fifth of it is covered by buildings. Statistics are calculated on GHSL's own equal-area grid, so every cell is exactly one hectare, with today's municipal boundaries for every year.

New land in each period is classed as infill, edge growth or leapfrog by the share of already built-up land around it (the landscape expansion index).

Data and tools

  • Data: GHSL built-up surface and population (European Commission JRC), SANLC 2022 (DFFE), municipal boundaries (MDB), OpenStreetMap
  • Tools: QGIS, Python with GDAL and NumPy, Leaflet, HTML, CSS and JavaScript

How the land was added

Most new building attached itself to existing settlement. Infill closes gaps inside it; edge growth extends it; leapfrog development starts a new patch with no built-up land around it.

Two Gautengs

Built area per person in 1975 and 2020. In the three metros it fell: people arrived faster than land was built up, so more people share each hectare. In Midvaal, Emfuleni and the West Rand it rose: land was built up faster than people arrived.

Roads, not rail

New built-up land sits close to the main road network far more often than chance would give. Near railway stations the opposite trend shows: growth there was strongest before 2000 and weaker since.

Checking the data, and its limits

Against South Africa's own land cover. SANLC 2022 (DFFE) agrees that 95% of the land GHSL calls built-up in 2020 is built-up. SANLC maps more land as built-up overall (4,168 km² against 2,443) because it counts whole suburbs, gardens included, so GHSL's figures are on the cautious side. GHSL's population for 2020, 15.5 million, is close to the 2022 Census count of about 15.1 million.

  • Only 1975, 1990, 2000, 2014 and 2018 come from satellite images (Landsat, then Sentinel-2). GHSL fills the years between by modelling, so the five-year steps are estimates, marked on the timeline.
  • The 1975 layer rests on coarse Landsat images; small settlements may be missed or placed loosely.
  • The "built-up land" threshold (a fifth of the hectare under buildings) is a choice. The totals of roofed area, which need no threshold, tell the same story: 532 to 1,113 km².
  • Roads and stations are as mapped today, not as they were in each year.
