Where can solar connect in the Northern Cape?

GeoReady looks past good sunshine to the question investors ask next: can a project actually connect to the grid, at what cost, and with what permits? It scores 2,132 candidate zones under three grid scenarios.

Preview. The cost and capacity assumptions have not yet been reviewed by an energy engineer or a grid planner.

What the model found

Land is not what holds solar back. About 184,000 km², half the province, passes every exclusion, enough for about 1,560 GW on a tenth of it.

The grid is. As the grid stands, every zone sits behind a local-area or supply-area limit of 0 MW. Nothing can connect.

Reinforcing the backbone would open about 4.3 GW, in 18 zones. Eight substations, including Upington, Kronos and Paulputs, would still take 0 MW, and 45% of the potential sits behind them.

Cost barely separates zones. Nine in ten fall between R807 and R886/MWh. Grid headroom is what separates them.

Readiness weighs sun (0.20), cost (0.25), distance to the grid (0.15), grid headroom (0.25) and being in a solar REDZ (0.15), each on a fixed 0–1 scale. The weights matter: weighting sun most, only 14 of the top 50 zones stay in the top 50; weighting grid or permitting most, 46 and 43 do.

How the model works

Five Jupyter notebooks on a small Python package. Every assumption sits in one settings file with its source, every input is checksummed, and nothing is written unless the release checks pass. The same notebooks run in plain Jupyter and inside QGIS with the Notebook plugin, where the last step loads the results onto your map.

  • 00_run_record.ipynbRun record: parameters, input checksums, software
  • 01_eligibility_zones.ipynbExclusion reasons and candidate zones
  • 02_capacity_cost.ipynbCapacity, yield, connection cost and LCOE
  • 03_grid_readiness.ipynbGrid limits, allocation, supply curves, readiness and sensitivity
  • 04_qa_publish.ipynbRelease checks, outputs, and loading the results into QGIS
How cost is estimated, and how it compares

Levelised cost in real 2025 rand, from plant CAPEX anchored on IRENA's 2024 cost data, Eskom's line and substation building blocks for the connection, and the 8.2% real discount rate used in the IRP. The low case (median R537/MWh) lands near the R500–514/MWh that Bid Window 7 solar projects bid. The base case (median R847) is higher because it assumes fixed panels, where new plants track the sun, and cautious CAPEX. Read the cost as relative: which zones are cheaper holds better than the rand figure.

What this is not

  • Not a permit or a feasibility study. A readiness score is not an environmental authorisation, a land right, an engineering design or a bankable study.
  • Not a connection offer. GCCA 2025 limits are indicative and dated. They are shared with projects outside the province, which are not modelled, so every allocation is an upper bound.
  • Distances are straight lines to the nearest substation of 132 kV or more in OpenStreetMap, scaled up for routing; some substations may be missing.
  • Thresholds are choices. The 50 km cap, the 15° slope limit and the 10% land-use factor can be changed in the settings file; only protected areas are treated as off-limits by law. The province by reason:
    • Eligible: 183,874 km² (49%)
    • Protected area (legal): 20,488 km² (5%)
    • Incompatible land cover: 6,688 km² (2%)
    • Too steep: 6,110 km² (2%)
    • Weak solar resource: 4,827 km² (1%)
    • Too far from a substation: 150,906 km² (40%)
